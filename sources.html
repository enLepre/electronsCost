Sources

Where the numbers come from.

Every preset price below comes from Cost of electricity.xlsx, the workbook used to build this calculator. The amounts are the original workbook inputs, not live market quotations. No quotation dates are recorded.

Five reagent entries include external links. The other reagent prices and all electricity prices have no external reference recorded. A link identifies the workbook’s stated source; it does not verify that the linked page supports the exact amount shown.

Reagent prices

Purchase prices in USD/kg. Molar masses and reaction-specific electron counts convert these to a cost per electron equivalent. Corrected inputs are identified alongside their original workbook values.

Electricity prices

Prices in USD/kWh. Repeated workbook rows at different voltages are consolidated into one source preset; voltage is chosen independently in the calculator. Generation costs (LCOE/VALCOE) are not necessarily delivered electricity tariffs.

Conversions & custom inputs

From purchase price to electron cost

The workbook’s Chemical column D multiplies price by molar mass and divides by 1000. Column F divides that molar cost by electron equivalents. The calculator also applies the utilization set in Details, then divides by the Avogadro constant for USD per individual electron equivalent.

Physical constants

Faraday constant: 96,485.33212 C/mol. Avogadro constant: 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. One kWh is exactly 3,600,000 J. See NIST CODATA physical constants.

Chemistry corrections

Oxygen uses the molecular mass of O₂, 31.9988 g/mol, from the NIST Chemistry WebBook, rather than the workbook’s 16 g/mol. Ammonia oxidation to N₂ uses 3 electron equivalents per NH₃, obtained by balancing 2 NH₃ → N₂ + 6 H⁺ + 6 e⁻. These corrections change the cost per electron, not the recorded purchase prices.

Other molar masses and electron counts are retained from the workbook without independent verification. The adiponitrile transformation is unspecified. Sodium borohydride is identified from the workbook’s NaBH₄ molar mass. See Assumptions for the reaction basis and limits.

Custom and edited values

Custom inputs and edits on Details are user-supplied assumptions. This catalogue continues to show the original presets. The custom editor starts with example values (1 USD/kg, 100 g/mol and 2 electron equivalents for a reagent; 0.1 USD/kWh for electricity). These starting values are placeholders, with no supplier or market source.

No source is inferred when the workbook supplies none. Replace undated or unsupported prices with comparable quotations before using the comparison for a purchasing or process decision.
