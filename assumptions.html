Assumptions & method

Cost of supplying one mole of useful electron equivalents.

Ideal case

Assume complete reagent utilization, 100% Faradaic efficiency and a lossless power supply. A superscript (0) denotes ideal quantities.

Chemical route

For reagent price P (USD kg⁻¹), molar mass M (g mol⁻¹), and n electron equivalents per mole of reagent, the chemical cost per mole of useful electrons is

Cchem(0) = PM / (1000 n)
(1)

Cchem(0) is in USD (mol e⁻)⁻¹; 1000 converts grams to kilograms.

Electrical route

With Faraday constant F = 96,485.33212 C mol⁻¹ and operating cell voltage V (V), the purchased energy per mole of useful electrons, E(0) (J mol⁻¹), is

E(0) = FV
(2)

For electricity price p (USD kWh⁻¹) and conversion factor κ = 3,600,000 J kWh⁻¹, the electrical cost per mole is

Celec(0) = p E(0) / κ = pFV / κ
(3)

Celec(0) is in USD (mol e⁻)⁻¹. “Ideal” concerns utilization and conversion efficiencies; V remains the chosen full cell voltage.

Break-even voltage

For p > 0, equal molar costs define the ideal break-even voltage V₀*:

V₀* = κ Cchem(0) / (pF) = κ PM / (1000 pF n)
(4)

Electricity is cheaper when V < V₀*.

Realistic case

Introduce reagent utilization u, Faradaic efficiency ηF, and power-supply efficiency ηPS. Each is a fraction between zero (excluded) and one.

Chemical route

Only the fraction u of reagent electron equivalents is useful. The corrected chemical cost Cchem is

Cchem = Cchem(0) / u = PM / (1000 n u)
(5)

Electrical route

The required charge per mole of useful electrons, Q (C mol⁻¹), increases as Faradaic efficiency decreases:

Q = F / ηF
(6)

The purchased energy per mole, E (J mol⁻¹), additionally includes power-supply losses:

E = QV / ηPS = E(0) / (ηF ηPS)
(7)

The corrected electrical cost Celec is therefore

Celec = Celec(0) / (ηF ηPS) = pFV / (κ ηF ηPS)
(8)

Both corrected costs retain units of USD (mol e⁻)⁻¹.

Break-even voltage

Setting the corrected costs equal defines V*, the realistic break-even voltage:

V* = V₀* ηF ηPS / u
(9)

Electricity is cheaper when V < V*. Setting u = ηF = ηPS = 1 recovers the ideal result.

Cost per electron · both cases

For route i ∈ {chem, elec}, let Ci be molar cost and ci cost per individual electron equivalent. With Avogadro constant NA = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹,

ci = Ci / NA
(10)

The comparison graph displays ci in USD/e⁻. For N moles of useful electron equivalents, total cost is N Ci.

Shared assumptions

At p=0, electricity costs zero at every voltage; no unique break-even voltage exists.

P, p, V ≥ 0; M, n > 0; 0 < u, ηF, ηPS ≤ 1. Utilization and efficiencies are fractions (100% = 1), held constant over the voltage range.

V includes cell losses. This cost comparison does not establish reaction feasibility. Other reagents, the paired electrode reaction, equipment, separation and waste costs are excluded.

Reaction basis

O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ → 2 H₂On=4; M=31.9988 g mol⁻¹

2 NH₃ → N₂ + 6 H⁺ + 6 e⁻n=3 per NH₃

Workbook mode restores M=16 for oxygen and n=6 for ammonia. Adiponitrile retains an unverified n=6. See Sources for provenance and Details to edit inputs.
