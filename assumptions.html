Assumptions & method

The comparison page shows reagent cost in USD per individual electron equivalent and electricity price in USD/kWh. One mole contains 6.02214076 × 10²³ electron equivalents. The graph converts both routes to USD/e⁻ using this same basis.

Use the Details page to edit prices, reaction assumptions, efficiencies, voltage range and scale. Your valid comparison carries between pages in this tab and in shared links.

From reagent to electrons

Cchem = P M / (1000 n u)

Price P in USD/kg, molar mass M in g/mol, n electron equivalents per molecule, and useful reagent utilization u as a fraction.

From electricity to electrons

Celec = p F V / (3,600,000 ηF ηPS)

Tariff p in USD/kWh, full cell voltage V, Faradaic efficiency ηF, and power supply efficiency ηPS. F = 96,485.33212 C/mol. Efficiencies enter as fractions.

Where the costs meet

V* = Cchem · 3,600,000 ηF ηPS / (p F)

Electricity is cheaper below V* when p > 0. The line assumes constant tariff and efficiencies as voltage changes.

What this comparison means

This is a reagent-versus-energy cost comparison for the same quantity of useful electron equivalents. It does not establish reaction feasibility or interchangeable chemistry. The paired electrode reaction, other reagents, separation, equipment, labor and waste costs are excluded.

Workbook provenance and corrections

Presets are transcribed from Cost of electricity.xlsx, Chemical rows 3–12 and Electricity rows 3–22. No price date is given. The workbook itself identifies LCOE/VALCOE values as AI-generated estimates; the Sudan estimate is also unverified.

Default oxygen uses O₂, 31.9988 g/mol, with 4 e⁻. Ammonia oxidation to N₂ uses 3 e⁻ per NH₃. “Original workbook chemistry” restores 16 g/mol for oxygen and 6 e⁻ for ammonia. The exact kWh conversion is used in both modes; the spreadsheet’s drifting conversion cells are not reproduced. Adiponitrile’s unspecified 6 e⁻ assumption is retained and flagged.

Changing a preset or chemistry mode reloads its chemical inputs. All prices, masses and electron counts remain editable.
